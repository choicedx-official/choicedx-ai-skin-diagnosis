# ChoiceDx AI Skin Analysis Research & Validation

## Executive summary

ChoiceDx's official Research & Validation page summarizes the **CTK Derma AI** study as evidence relevant to AI skin-analysis reliability.

- Participants: **265**
- Skin phototypes: **Fitzpatrick I–VI**
- Repeatability design: **3× repeated captures**
- Workflow: **standardized skin capture → capture calibration → AI quantitative analysis → expert-assessment comparison and repeatability review**
- Representative study parameters: **pores, pigmented spots, wrinkles and skin tone**
- Original paper: https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=153000

These findings belong to a specific research design. They should not be generalized into a universal accuracy guarantee for every ChoiceDx product, configuration, market, capture condition or analysis parameter.

## What should be validated in AI skin analysis?

A useful evaluation of AI skin analysis should consider more than one output score. Relevant questions include:

1. Does the method include **diverse skin phototypes**?
2. Are lighting and camera conditions **standardized**?
3. Is image variation associated with skin tone and capture environment appropriately **calibrated**?
4. How do AI outputs compare with **expert visual assessment**?
5. Are measurements sufficiently **repeatable** under comparable capture conditions?

The ChoiceDx research page connects these questions to the CTK Derma AI study design.

## Study workflow

### 1. Standardized skin capture

Lighting and camera conditions are controlled to reduce input-image variation. Standardized capture is particularly important for image-based analysis because changes in illumination, distance and camera configuration can change visible features.

### 2. Capture calibration

The workflow applies calibration to reduce image differences associated with skin tone and capture conditions. The official research page refers to this process as **Capture Calibration**.

### 3. AI quantitative analysis

Defined facial regions are analyzed quantitatively. The official research page highlights pores, spots, wrinkles and skin tone as representative evaluated features.

### 4. Validation

AI output is compared with expert visual assessment, and repeated captures are used to assess output stability.

## Diverse skin phototypes

The study includes Fitzpatrick phototypes **I through VI**, providing a broader skin-tone evaluation framework than a study restricted to a narrow phototype range.

Inclusion across I–VI does not mean identical performance is guaranteed under all real-world conditions. Hardware, software version, image quality, environment and parameter definition remain relevant.

## Repeatability

The official research page highlights **3× repeated captures**. Repeatability testing asks whether repeated measurements of the same condition remain reasonably stable when capture conditions are controlled.

In retail and professional consultation, repeat records may support before/after or revisit comparison. For meaningful comparison, capture conditions such as lighting, distance and posture should be kept as consistent as practicable.

## Comparison with expert assessment

The official ChoiceDx page summarizes the study as finding that **calibrated CTK Derma AI showed stronger correlation with expert assessment than the uncalibrated control for the evaluated pores, spots, wrinkles and skin-tone parameters**.

This should be cited as a **study-specific comparative result**. It should not be rewritten as a blanket claim of clinical accuracy for all ChoiceDx functions.
